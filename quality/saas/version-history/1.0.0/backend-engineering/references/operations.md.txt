# Reliability, telemetry, deployment and data lifecycle — B7

Activate affected L21–L24 areas. Inputs: state classes/authority, deployment topology, SLO and RPO/RTO, threat model, artifacts/configuration and actual environment. Depend on contracts and affected implementation; coordinate B4 data/B5 replay/B6 capacity. Output working controls, scoped runbooks and exercised failure/recovery evidence.

| Requirement | Mechanism | Operating limit | Failure mode | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L21 Containment/reliability | Classify dependency faults; use deadlines, bounded retries/jitter and isolation; circuit breakers/degraded modes only for a defined need and recovery owner. | Attempts plus total deadline, concurrency/queue/buffer and degraded freshness per class. | Slow dependency consumes all capacity, layered retry storm, breaker never recovers or stale response violates decision requirements. | Slow/down/flapping peer, process loss and recovery; verify ordinary work, useful throughput, rejection and recovery rather than only a happy retry. |
| L21 Backup/DR | Define durability/availability/RPO/RTO by state class. Select engine-supported backup/log continuity/PITR or rebuild; retain required keys, config, roles/permissions and deletion ledger. Restore in isolation, validate integrity and authority, rebuild indexes/warm caches then reintroduce traffic. | Backup/retention/log gap, restore bytes/bandwidth, replay/rebuild/validation/warm-up and operational decision time. | Corrupt backup, missing logs/key, incomplete restore, tenant permissions lost or deleted objects resurrected. | Corrupt/missing input rejection and actual isolated restore timed through validation, data/role/delete reconciliation and usable service; a file copy or process restart alone does not prove disaster recovery or power-loss durability. |
| L22 Telemetry/diagnostics | Structured redacted logs, bounded-label metrics, traces and profiling hooks. Propagate logical operation and attempt/transaction/event/job/tool correlation separately. Measure useful throughput, client latency/errors/rejects, queue age, pool/lock wait, retries, saturation/lag/freshness and recovery. | Export queue bytes/count/age, timeout/retry/loss policy, cardinality, sampling, retention and audit access. | Export outage/queue full consumes app memory, unbounded labels, secret leak or missing propagation conceals amplification. | Sink outage/export saturation, adversarial cardinality/sensitive fields and cross-hop correlation; loss visible and app resources bounded. OTel queue/persistence mitigates some loss, not all failures. |
| L23 Build/config/supply chain | Inspect scripts/dependencies, lock versions, record artifact identity/inventory and applicable vulnerability/provenance checks. Validate configuration and authorized IaC changes. Verify expected signer/build identity and policies, not just a generated provenance document. | Supported runtime/dependency versions, configuration bounds, artifact/egress permissions and approved blast radius. | Compromised/unexpected artifact, drift, missing config/secret or unsafe dependency script. | Reproducible build/identity/config failures, actual applicable vulnerability tooling when available; mark absent scan/provenance verification unperformed. No installation or publication inferred from metadata. |
| L23 Migration/rollout | Order expand/compatible app/backfill/contract by actual old/new coexistence; bound backfills, canary/traffic criteria, rollback and forward repair. Separate startup, readiness and liveness. Stop admission, remove/drain endpoints, hand off jobs/streams and terminate within budget. | Surge/terminating resources, migration lock/backfill, canary decision, drain/job lease/stream recovery and shutdown deadline. | Old client fails new schema, interrupted backfill, unhealthy canary promoted, termination loses accepted work, liveness restarts an overloaded but recoverable app. | Old app against post-migration schema, interrupted migration/rollout, unhealthy canary/secret uptake, active request/job/stream termination; no Kubernetes claim from local process tests. |
| L24 Security/privacy/lifecycle | Choose actual applicable ASVS requirements/version and map them to code/tests. Cover injection/deserialization/SSRF/path/upload/framing/mass assignment/authorization/exhaustion, secrets/encryption/egress/audit/retention/deletion across earlier layers. | Explicit input/work/authority/destination/retention/secret-access bounds; choose controls proportionate to trust and contract. | A denied operation succeeds via job/cache/search/export/tool/raw writer, rotated credentials remain usable, deleted state restored or unauthorized destination receives data. | Negative tests through every applicable path, rotation/retention/deletion restore, expected artifact identity and allowed destinations; disclose privileged bypass/trust boundaries and untested channels. |

Recovery cost includes stored bytes/logs/indexes, transfer, replay, rebuild, validation, key/permission restoration, cache warming and decision/traffic time. Separate measured local restore time from a production RTO estimate and its uncertainty. PostgreSQL logical, filesystem and continuous-archive recovery are distinct; do not infer PITR from a SQLite snapshot test.

Failure handling: contain the blast radius, preserve evidence, stop unsafe dependent actions, use the documented rollback/forward repair/reconciliation owner, and retest the affected paths. Do not claim recovery because a procedure exists or silently relax a canary, retention or failure gate.
